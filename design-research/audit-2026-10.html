RCCG Jesus House, Middletown

Jesus House Audit

The site looks handmade and the copy is warm. But a first-time visitor on a phone has to scroll through about 18 screens, several of them blank, to reach what a church site has to answer up front: when, where, and how to get there. I recommend a redesign that keeps the seal, the voice, and the illustrations, retires the pinned zoom journey, and rebuilds the homepage around this coming Sunday.

The short version

7 of 19Phone screens in a scroll-through that show nothing to read: blank paper or a zoomed fragment of art.
0Structured-data blocks. Google gets no machine-readable address, phone, or service time.

The craft is real. The July performance campaign took a Galaxy A15 from 11 fps to about 80 fps, and Lighthouse scores accessibility at 100. The problem is the brief. A parish with no other web presence needs a front door that answers questions in one screen. The zoom journey makes people earn every answer.

Findings

Ranked by how much each one costs a visitor or the parish. Each finding names what I saw and the fix.

Critical

Blocks a first visit

The answers sit at the bottom of a 16,000 px page

Saw
The hero gives "9:00 AM" and "Washington Street" in one sentence. The full address, directions, and the "what Sunday is like" details arrive only after five pinned scenes.
Fix
Put date, time, full address, a directions button, and the ride request in the first phone screen. Treat everything after it as optional reading.

A third of the phone scroll is empty

Saw
Seven of nineteen phone screens show blank paper, a blank navy field, or art zoomed until it is abstract (a car window, a hymnal). A visitor unfamiliar with the concept may think the page failed to load.
Fix
Remove the pinned camera dives. Use the existing illustrations as static plates beside the copy they illustrate.

The ride request depends on a mail app, and the pickup spots do nothing

Saw
Every "Need a ride?" button opens mailto:. Many students use Gmail in a browser and have no mail app set up, so the tap does nothing. "Exley", "Usdan", "Sci-Li" and "Foss Hill" are styled like buttons but are plain <li> items. On phones the request button is still faded when the card leaves the screen.
Fix
Make the pickup spots real choices that fill in a ready-to-send message. Offer a text message first and email second, then build the Phase 3 form. The prefilled text can echo the pastor's own Sunday line.
High

Hurts finding the church or trusting the details

Every unknown URL returns the homepage with status 200

Saw
/robots.txt, /sitemap.xml, /about, /give and /events all return 200 with the 73 KB homepage. Lighthouse reads the homepage HTML as robots.txt and reports 23 errors.
Fix
Add a real 404 route with a 404 status, a plain-text robots.txt, and a sitemap.xml listing the three pages.

Search engines and WhatsApp get poor information

Saw
There is no Church JSON-LD. og:image is the relative path /social-card.png, and some link-preview crawlers need an absolute URL. The parish spreads news through WhatsApp, so the link preview is the first thing most people will see.
Fix
Add Church schema with address, geo, phone, and openingHoursSpecification for Sunday and Wednesday. Make every OG and Twitter image URL absolute. Test the preview by pasting the link into WhatsApp.

Unconfirmed facts are live

Saw
The site says 9:00 AM. Google lists Sunday 9 to 11 AM, and the chat history suggests a start nearer 10:00. Google also lists a Wednesday 6:30 PM service, which the site never mentions. The RCF Instagram handle is marked unconfirmed in the code.
Fix
Confirm the schedule with the pastors before any redesign work ships. A wrong service time costs more than any design flaw.

Things visitors look for are missing

Saw
No midweek service, no leaders' names, no online option (service moves to Zoom on snow days), no giving, and no events, even though the year has fixed moments like Thanksgiving Sunday, the September cookout, and the November African-attire service.
Fix
Add a Midweek block and a short About page with leaders (with their consent). The homepage can announce the next fixed event from a small hand-edited list until Sanity arrives.
Medium

Care, cost, and consistency

The zoom journey ignores Reduce Motion

Saw
The scene directives have no prefers-reduced-motion gate (a June decision). The dives scale art up to 23×, which is the kind of motion that setting exists to stop. WCAG 2.3.3 is AAA, so this does not fail AA; Lighthouse does not measure the motion.
Fix
Retiring the dives removes the problem. Any motion that stays should be small, transform-only, and honor the setting.

The scroll engine is expensive to keep alive

Saw
Five registered scroll properties, view-timeline keyframes that must live in the global stylesheet, and per-scene timing tables. July took a dozen journal entries of device profiling. The project's own rule says the site "must survive years of light attention", and the Phase 2 CMS content will not fit into fixed pinned scenes.
Fix
Delete scene.directive.ts, scroll-scrubber.ts, dark-ground.ts and the scroll-driven block in styles.css once the new homepage lands.

Smaller defects

Saw
On a short laptop window (1440×722) the scroll cue sits on top of "Plan your visit". "Neighbors" and "neighbours" both appear on the homepage. Lighthouse Best Practices is 77 because of a third-party cookie and logged browser issues. The July Lighthouse run measured mobile LCP at 7.3 s on simulated 4G. I did not re-measure that today.
Fix
Pick American spelling throughout. Re-run the performance trace after the redesign. A plain page with one hero image should reach LCP well under 2.5 s.

The site breaks your own UI rules

Saw
The page background is #FAF7EF, a warm cream. Labels use middle-dot separators ("RCCG · Middletown, Connecticut", "Come and see · John 1:46"). Letter-spaced eyebrow labels sit over most headings. Your code-style rules ban all three.
Fix
Move to a cool paper white, write labels as plain phrases, and let headings stand alone.

What to keep

  • The RCCG seal and its sampled colorsDiaspora visitors recognize it at once.
  • The ride ministry as the signatureIt came from 15 months of the pastor's Sunday-morning messages, and no other parish site in the network offers it.
  • The voice"No car? No problem." and "You'll be greeted at the door, never singled out." are specific and kind.
  • The Plan a Visit pageIt is clear and complete, and it is the most useful page on the site. The new homepage should borrow its plainness.
  • The illustration setThe meetinghouse, road, pew, and window scenes are already compressed WebP. They work well as still plates.
  • The foundationsServer-rendered text that reads with JavaScript off, a skip link, Fraunces with Mulish, and a privacy line that never lists members.

Redesign: This Sunday

The parish chat's most repeated Sunday line comes from the pastor arranging rides:

"Good morning, when you are ready let me know."Sent most Sunday mornings, March 2025 to May 2026

So the homepage becomes this coming Sunday. It knows the date. It says when it is Thanksgiving Sunday (the first Sunday of the month) and, in late November, that the dress code is African attire. It gives the time, the address, directions, and a ride request that reads like that message. All of this fits on one phone screen.

The swap test passes. A generic church site would not open with "Ride from Usdan" or "Thanksgiving Sunday". These details belong to this parish. The concept changes both page structure and appearance. The nav follows the week (This Sunday, Ride, Midweek, RCF, About) instead of brochure sections.

The rest of the homepage: the morning, in order

Below the first screen, one short timeline replaces the five pinned scenes. Each step gets one illustration plate and two lines of copy. The times come from the current Plan a Visit page. Pickup times need confirming.

  1. before 9:00Pickups from campusRoad scene. Pickup spots and how to ask.
  2. a little before 9Doors openMeetinghouse scene. Greeted, never singled out.
  3. 9:00Worship and the WordPew scene. About 90 minutes, come as you are.
  4. after serviceFellowshipWindow scene. Students and neighbors together.
  5. afterwardRides backThe car again, heading home.

The red car stays as the one motion moment. It moves along this timeline as the reader scrolls, using transforms only, and stands still under Reduce Motion. Midweek, RCF, and the next fixed event follow, then the footer.

Changes

  • Pinned zoom journey becomes a five-step morning timeline
  • Cream ground becomes a cool paper white tinted toward seal navy
  • Eyebrows and middle dots become plain headings and phrases
  • The ride CTA becomes a pickup chooser with a prefilled message
  • The navy "cathedral" band stays, used once, for the story
  • The RCF hero dive becomes the same plate treatment

Stays

  • Seal, palette, Fraunces and Mulish
  • Illustration plates, door Lottie, and dove
  • Plan a Visit content and layout
  • Angular SSR on Vercel, no CMS until Phase 2
  • No member names or photos without consent

Plan

  1. Confirm the facts with the parish

    Get answers to the questions below. Nothing from step 3 ships until the schedule is confirmed.

    A conversation to plan a ride.

  2. Fix the current site now

    Real 404 status, robots.txt, sitemap.xml, Church JSON-LD, absolute OG image, pickup spots that prefill the ride email, spelling, and the scroll-cue overlap. These details help even if the larger redesign stays on hold.

    About a day. Five or fewer commits, straight to main after the usual checks.

  3. Rebuild the homepage as This Sunday

    Time-aware first screen, ride chooser, morning timeline with static plates, Midweek and next-event blocks, the new ground color, and RCF moved to the same treatment. Then delete the scene engine. Target: under six phone screens and mobile LCP under 2.5 s.

    More than five commits, so a feat/this-sunday branch with a preview deploy.

  4. Ride form, About, and photographs

    The Phase 3 ride form with notifications to the pastors, an About page with leaders who agree to appear, and a consented photo session of the building and a Sunday service. A real photo of the red doors then replaces the illustrated hero.

    Follows the existing roadmap phases 3 to 5.

Questions for the parish

  1. What time does Sunday service start: 9:00, as the site says, or later?
  2. Is the Wednesday 6:30 PM service on Google current? What is it called, and is it in person or online?
  3. Can (860) 518-4640 receive text messages, or should ride requests go to another number?
  4. What time do campus pickups usually start, and are Exley, Usdan, Sci-Li and Foss Hill still the right spots?
  5. Which leaders want to appear on an About page, and is the spelling Iweoise or Iweiose?
  6. Would the parish agree to a short photo session of the building and a service, with consent from anyone in frame?
  7. Does Wesleyan RCF have its own Instagram, or should the site keep pointing to @rcfcampus?
